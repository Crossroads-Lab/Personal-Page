# Diversity

## What's your view on diversity in a team?

I support it fully, and my reasoning is adaptive rather than decorative: our power comes from diversity, in the natural-selection sense.

A monoculture is efficient right up until the environment changes, and then it has no answer, because every member of it fails the same way at the same time. A varied population carries variation it isn't currently using — and that spare variation is the entire reason it survives a shift. Teams behave the same way.

So when I'm looking at a group of people who all trained the same way, worked at the same kind of company, and reach for the same tool first, I don't see a strong team. I see a team that is well adapted to the problem it already had.

## Isn't that just an argument about cognitive diversity?

It covers that, but it's broader, and I'd be wary of the dodge where "diversity of thought" is used to avoid the rest of it. People's backgrounds shape what they notice. Someone who has been the only person like them in a room notices things about that room that nobody else in it does.

I've had the experience from the inside in a small way — I arrived in the US as a French exchange student who had been told at home that he wouldn't amount to an engineer. Being the outsider in the room is not a disadvantage you overcome. It is frequently the thing you can see that nobody else can.

## How do you promote diversity, equity and inclusion in hiring and project assignment?

I'll answer the part I've actually practised and be clear about the part I haven't written up.

What I've changed deliberately is the interview. After we let someone go at Heali for a fit problem that was my mistake at hiring time, we built cultural-add evaluation into the process properly — explicit questions about how a person operates with no process, no support function and no clear brief — instead of treating fit as a feeling you form afterwards. That matters for this question because "fit" assessed by instinct is where bias does most of its work. Making the criteria explicit is what makes it reviewable.

On assignment, my bias is toward building teams that are multidisciplinary by construction, because the problems demanded it — engineers, product designers and UX researchers at Heali; business, design, data science, engineering and legal at Snap.

If you want the specifics of a formal DEI programme — targets, sourcing pipelines, structured panels — ask me directly rather than taking an answer from this page. I'd rather tell you what I have and haven't run than claim a practice I didn't build.

## What have your teams actually looked like?

Multidisciplinary by construction, because the problems demanded it. At Heali I recruited and directed a group of engineers, product designers and UX researchers working on nutrition science for 200+ medical conditions — you cannot build that with one kind of person. At Snap I coordinated across business, design, data science, engineering and legal.

Cross-domain knowledge is on my list of values for the same reason diversity is: the useful insight usually arrives from outside the discipline that owns the problem.
