# What I value

## What are your core values? What do you stand for?

The ones I've kept on every version of my resume: lead by example, fail and learn fast, be customer-centric, be data-driven, stay resilient, and be outspoken. Add creative, critical, open-minded, empathetic, and comfortable working across domains rather than down a single one.

If you made me cut that to one, it's leading by example. The rest are easier to hold when the person asking is visibly doing the thing too.

## Which one matters most to you, and why that one?

Leading by example, and it isn't abstract. If I'm asking my team to do something, I should be willing and able to do it myself — in the trenches with them, not describing the trenches from a distance.

It came from Amazon. They had a grid of leadership principles you'd work through — honestly, it felt like choose-your-own-adventure, or building out a character sheet in an RPG. You picked the ones you'd be measured against at review time, and they became the language your performance was discussed in.

Most of that framework I've left behind. That one stuck, and it's the one I still measure myself against.

## What does "fail and learn fast" mean in practice for you?

Ship the smallest real thing, put it in front of someone, and let it be wrong early. Thinking outside the box, focusing on MVPs, failing and iterating quickly, staying close to the user — that's the loop, and it's how I've worked from research through to founding.

The failure part is not a slogan. It only counts if you're willing to say out loud that the thing you built isn't working, before someone else has to point it out.

## What do you expect from the people who work for you?

The same thing I'm asking of myself: do the work, say the true thing early, and don't make someone else discover your problem for you. I'd much rather hear "this is going sideways and here's what I think we do" in week two than a clean status report in week two and a disaster in week six.

Being outspoken is on my list of values for that reason. I'd rather have the uncomfortable conversation while it's still cheap.
