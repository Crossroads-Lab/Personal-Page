# Being customer-centric

## What does customer-centric actually mean to you?

Predictability and reliability. Those are the pillars of trust, and trust is the thing customers are actually buying.

An excellent product is not always the most innovative one. That's an uncomfortable sentence for engineers and founders, because innovation is the part that's enjoyable and the part that gets talked about. But the product people depend on is the one that behaves the same way today as it did yesterday, and the novel thing that works most of the time loses to the boring thing that always works.

## Can you give an example of choosing reliability over innovation?

Most of my time at iScribeHealth. On paper, leading a company through SOC 2 and HIPAA compliance is not the exciting line on a roadmap — and it was the right thing to spend my time on.

We bulletproofed the core architecture for scalability and high availability, which cut production downtime by 70%. We built an incident recovery pipeline from detection through to communication. We took the platform from .NET 4 to .NET 8. We ran the company's first-ever penetration test and closed 100% of the critical and high-severity findings.

None of that is a feature anyone demos. All of it is the reason a clinician can rely on the system while a patient is sitting in front of them.

## Why does that matter more in healthcare?

Because the cost of unpredictability isn't churn, it's someone's appointment.

If our documentation system is flaky, a provider doesn't file a support ticket and wait — they go back to typing notes at 9pm, and they don't come back. You get one chance to be the thing they stop worrying about. Innovation can wait for the release after that; reliability cannot.

That's also why I'd rather deliver something narrower that holds than something broader that mostly holds. Scope is recoverable. Trust, once you've spent it, mostly isn't.

## Isn't that just risk aversion?

No, and I'd push back on the framing. It's about sequencing.

Do the unglamorous work first and you earn the right to take real swings later, because you have a platform that can absorb them. Skip it, and every subsequent innovation is built on something you're quietly hoping holds — which is a bet you make with the customer's experience rather than your own.
