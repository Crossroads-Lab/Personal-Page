# Where I'm from

## Where are you from? What's your background personally?

I'm French. I was born in Levallois-Perret, a suburb just outside Paris, and raised in Troyes, in the Champagne region. I came to the United States for graduate school and stayed. I'm now a US citizen.

## Where do you live? Where are you based?

I live in Reno, Nevada.

## How did you end up in the United States?

I came to Oregon State University in 2004 as a French exchange student. The plan was one year. I stayed seven, finished a PhD in computer science in 2011, and never really left the country after that.

I'll be honest about how I picked the school: of the universities on my shortlist, Oregon State was the closest one to the ocean. I wanted to surf.

## What do you do outside of work? What are your hobbies?

Cooking, and time with my family — those are the ones that actually hold the week together.

Then surfing, skiing, and DJing. I've DJ'd professionally and promoted events, so that one is less of a hobby than a parallel life. The surfing is what got me to Oregon in the first place, and living in Reno means the skiing is about forty minutes away. Also running, swimming, and I'm an unapologetic movie nerd.

## How do you disconnect and recharge?

Cooking, mostly. It's the one thing I do where the feedback loop is immediate, the scope is bounded, and nobody is going to ask me for a roadmap. You start, you finish, people eat it.

That and family time, which I protect more deliberately than I used to. I wrote about this in my book chapter — the dinner where everyone puts their phones away, and how unreasonable it feels to even ask. Being the person who asks anyway is part of what I think recharging actually is now.

Then the physical ones: surfing, skiing, running, swimming. Anything where the problem in front of me is not a problem I can think my way out of.

## Do you speak other languages?

French is my first language. I grew up in France and came to the US in 2004 as an exchange student — the PhD was not the plan.
