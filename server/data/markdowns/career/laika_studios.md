# Laika Studios

## Did you ever work in animation or visual effects?

Yes — I was a Technical Director Intern at Laika, the stop-motion studio in Portland, Oregon, from June to August 2007. This was during the PhD, and it was the closest I got to the career I originally wanted.

## What did you actually build at Laika?

Tooling for the production pipeline rather than anything on screen.

I wrote Maya plug-ins against the Maya API, and Perl scripting inside the movie production pipeline. The main piece was a rendering system for real-time visualisation of heavy computer-generated video — a lightweight preview of a scene so directors could check and validate the animation without waiting on a full render.

That's the job in miniature: the creative people need to see the thing to judge it, and the thing is too expensive to see. You build the cheap approximation that's faithful enough to decide on.

## Why does that internship still matter to you?

Because it's the proof that the CGI ambition wasn't idle. I didn't just admire the industry from a distance — I worked in it, on exactly the kind of technology I'd wanted to build.

It also turned out to be good training for everything after. Real-time approximation of something expensive, built so a non-engineer can make a decision from it, describes a surprising amount of what I've done since.
