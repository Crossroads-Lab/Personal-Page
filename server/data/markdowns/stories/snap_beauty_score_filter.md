# The beauty score filter at Snap

## The situation

While I was a PM at Snap, one of my engineers wanted to ship a group AR filter built on a "beauty score." You'd take a selfie with friends and it would put a crown on whoever the model scored as most attractive. The technology was already built and working.

## What concerned me

Snap's audience skews young. A feature that publicly ranks teenagers by attractiveness, inside a friend group, is a product decision with real consequences for the people using it — and a serious brand risk for the company.

## What I did instead of blocking it

I didn't kill it outright. Killing an engineer's finished work is how you teach a team to stop bringing you things. I raised the concern and took the filter to design for feedback, so the objection came back as product input rather than as a veto from management.

## How it resolved

Design reached the same conclusion, and we redirected the underlying technology toward ad targeting, where the same model had genuine value without pointing it at teenagers. We avoided a PR crisis, the work wasn't wasted, and the engineer was promoted along the way.

## Why I tell this one

It's the clearest example of how I handle a call where the technology works but shipping it would be a mistake. The goal isn't to be the person who says no. It's to route the decision to the people whose judgment will carry it, and to find the version of the work that survives.
