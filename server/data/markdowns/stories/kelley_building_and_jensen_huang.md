# The Kelley building opening

## What's your best story from grad school?

I was one of a handful of students picked to attend the inauguration of the Kelley Engineering Center at Oregon State, chosen on grades and research. There was a dinner, and then the opening itself.

The part I've never forgotten came the next day. Jensen Huang — Nvidia's co-founder and CEO, and an Oregon State engineering graduate himself — got up and made an additional donation to the building, live, in front of everyone. He wrote it out with roughly the energy of someone paying for groceries.

I was a graduate student at the time. Watching money move at that scale, that casually, reorganised my sense of what was normal.

## Did you ever meet Jensen Huang?

Not properly — but he came through the graphics lab a few times handing out Nvidia goodies, which in hindsight makes complete sense: his gift to the building also named the Nvidia Graphics and Image Technologies Laboratory inside it. We were, quite literally, sitting in the lab his donation had paid for.

He graduated from Oregon State in electrical engineering in 1984, and met his wife there. The university has stayed a cause of his — he's given a great deal more to it since.

## What did that teach you?

Two things, and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is that the distance between a research lab and the money that makes it possible is shorter than it looks from inside the lab. Someone decided that room should exist.

The second is that he was an engineering graduate from the same programme I was sitting in. There was no special category of person involved. That's either reassuring or alarming, depending on the day.
