# The night the services went dark

## Tell me about a critical decision you made under severe time pressure

At iScribeHealth our services went dark. It was seven in the evening, which meant we had the whole night before our customers woke up and found out — a deadline that was both generous and completely fixed.

The technical decision matters less than the first one I actually made, which was about people.

## What did you do in the first hour?

I picked my two strongest engineers, called them, and then had food delivered to their homes so they could eat properly and stay up with me through the night.

That sounds like a small thing. It is the whole lesson. We had one shot at a fixed deadline and the binding constraint wasn't our diagnosis, it was whether three people could still think clearly at four in the morning. Feeding them was the highest-leverage decision available to me in that first hour.

I stayed up with them. I was not going to ask two people to burn a night on my behalf and then go to bed.

## What's the principle you took from that?

Put the human in the loop when the pressure is highest. Under time pressure the instinct is to optimise the technical path and treat the people executing it as a constant — and they are not a constant. They are the thing that degrades first.

So before I optimise the fix, I make sure the people doing it can last the distance: who is actually sharpest on this, can they eat, do they know what the deadline really is, and am I in it with them or directing from outside.

## Did you fix it?

We did, before the customers woke up. Nobody on their side ever experienced the outage.

That's the version of leading by example I care about. Not a speech about commitment — delivering dinner to someone's door at eight in the evening because they're about to give you their whole night.
