# Onboarding my own director

## Tell me about onboarding a senior or executive hire

The one I'd point to is unusual: at Snap I onboarded my second director — David Salesin, who came in to lead Snap Research in 2017.

So this wasn't onboarding someone into my team. It was onboarding the person who would be running the organisation I worked in. That inverts the normal dynamic, and I think it's the more interesting version of the question.

## What does onboarding someone more senior than you actually involve?

Context, not instruction. He didn't need me to explain research, or leadership, or how to run a lab — he'd founded Adobe's Creative Technologies Lab and had been a professor at UW since 1992. What he couldn't have was two years of accumulated, unwritten knowledge about this specific organisation: which projects had history, where the real decisions got made, which relationships were load-bearing, what had already been tried and quietly abandoned.

That's the thing nobody writes down, and it's the only thing a senior hire genuinely can't bring with them. My job was to transfer it quickly and without editorialising — give him the map, including the parts that didn't flatter us, and let him form his own judgements.

It also requires being honest about your own position. I had opinions about where the research org should go. The useful move was to state them clearly once, as input, and then not spend the next six months relitigating them through him.

## Was there anything personal about that one?

More than I expected. Early in his career, before Microsoft and Adobe, he'd worked at Lucasfilm and Pixar on animation that won Academy Awards.

I'd wanted to build technology for animation studios. That was the original ambition — the reason I went into graphics, the reason I interned at Laika. I ended up in AI by a sequence of accidents, and then years later found myself walking my new director through an org chart, knowing he'd actually had the career I'd been aiming at.

I never made that weird for him. But it's the closest my working life has come to a full circle.
